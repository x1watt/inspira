# Inspira stories: brief

Inspira is an app of historical wisdom. Besides quotes it now gets short
pieces a user reads in one or two minutes and learns something from. Three
kinds, in the same five categories as the quotes:

- `story`: fables, parables, folk tales, koans, and classic public domain
  short stories or literary episodes (Aesop, Panchatantra, Jataka, Zen and
  Sufi tales, Nasreddin, Chinese idiom stories, Bible and Talmud parables,
  Hasidic tales, Tolstoy, Chekhov, O. Henry, Andersen, Grimm, La Fontaine...).
  Retold in your own words.
- `history`: true or traditionally reported episodes from history, from
  antiquity to about 2000, all regions of the world (not only Greece and
  Rome): commanders, rulers, scientists, inventors, explorers, families,
  companies, expeditions, sports teams, rescues.
- `study`: published psychology, behavioral economics, sociology or
  organizational research findings (classic experiments, field studies,
  longitudinal studies, meta-analyses). Real, citable, well known in the
  field. Include famous ones with replication problems only with an honest
  caveat.

Categories (each item goes in exactly one):

- `relations`: friendship, trust, kindness, empathy, persuasion, conflict
  between people, reciprocity, how to treat people.
- `leadership`: command, example, responsibility, power and its abuse,
  decisions under pressure, motivating others, integrity of leaders.
- `team`: cooperation, unity, roles, group dynamics, collective effort,
  conformity and groupthink, psychological safety, crews and expeditions.
- `family`: parents and children, marriage, siblings, upbringing, home,
  generations, grief and care within families.
- `strategy`: planning, timing, deception, foresight, negotiation, decision
  making and its biases, competition, adaptation, war and business strategy.

Quality rules:
- Only items you know well. Never invent a story, a study, a statistic, a
  date or a citation. If unsure of a detail, leave it out.
- History: say where the account comes from when it is an anecdote
  ("Plutarch tells...", "According to Herodotus..."). Mark legends as
  legends.
- No duplicates: one story, episode or study appears once, under one key.
